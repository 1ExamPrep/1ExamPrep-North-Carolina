How do you know which license you need?

Start with the trade and the size of the job. General contracting on a project of $40,000 or more needs an NCLBGC license. Electrical contracting needs a license from the electrical board at any value. Plumbing, heating, fuel piping and fire sprinkler work needs the Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler board. The licensing guide walks through each path step by step.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions North Carolina candidates ask us most, checked against the state boards and statutes on November 24, 2026.

How many North Carolina contractor licenses are there?
The three boards issue dozens of classifications. This site covers 58 of them, every one we stock prep material for.
Which board licenses HVAC contractors?
The State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors, through Heating Groups 1, 2 and 3.
Can I hold more than one classification?
Yes. An NCLBGC license can carry several classifications. You pass the trade exam for each one.
Is there a statewide journeyman electrician license?
No. The electrical board licenses electrical contractors. Journeyman credentials depend on the employer or local program.
Do all NCLBGC licenses need the Business and Law exam?
Yes. Every NCLBGC applicant takes the Business and Law exam along with the trade exam.
Where do I take the exams?
PSI gives the NCLBGC exams. The electrical board gives its own written exams, and the Plumbing Board tests at 7 centers across the state.
