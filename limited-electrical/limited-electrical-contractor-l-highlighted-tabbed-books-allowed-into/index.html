How does it fit the North Carolina Limited Electrical Contractor (L) exam?

A Limited electrical license covers single projects up to the value the Board sets, which the statute caps at $100,000, on installations rated 1,000 volts or less. Limited applicants need no more than 3,000 hours of experience, at least 2,000 of them in direct installation work, under G.S. 87-43.3.

Candidates for this license prepare for 1 exam step:

  • Board Limited Electrical Contractor (L) exam: NEC lookups, calculations and North Carolina electrical rules (Written exam given by the electrical board)

What will you study with it?

The National Electrical Code, NEC, 2020 supports preparation for electrical definitions, branch circuits, feeders, services, wiring methods, conductors, raceways, boxes, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, disconnecting means, special equipment, special occupancies, and general installation requirements. NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm Code, 2019 supports preparation for fire alarm systems, signaling requirements, initiating devices, notification appliances, fire alarm control units, power supplies, documentation, inspection concepts, testing concepts, and fire alarm code navigation. Candidates pursuing the Limited classification often have hands-on experience, but the exam requires the ability to work from written code requirements. Field experience helps candidates understand real installations, but the test requires accurate code interpretation and efficient reference use. The highlighted and tabbed format helps candidates build familiarity with major sections, common topics, and important reference locations before they sit for the exam.
