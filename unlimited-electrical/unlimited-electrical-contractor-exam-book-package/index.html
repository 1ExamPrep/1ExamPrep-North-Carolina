What is in the Unlimited Electrical Contractor Exam Book Package?

The included references support study of services, feeders, branch circuits, motors, transformers, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, fire alarm systems, load calculations, plans, specifications, contractor licensing law, Board rules, business practices, and project management. The North Carolina Unlimited electrical contractor classification is the state’s broadest regular electrical contracting license. How does it fit the North Carolina Unlimited Electrical Contractor (U) exam?

An Unlimited electrical license lets a contractor take any electrical project in North Carolina, regardless of value. The Unlimited exam is the hardest of the three, with heavy National Electrical Code lookups and calculations, so a tabbed and highlighted NEC pays for itself.

Candidates for this license prepare for 1 exam step:

  • Board Unlimited Electrical Contractor (U) exam: NEC lookups, calculations and North Carolina electrical rules (Written exam given by the electrical board)

What will you study with it?

Unlimited electrical work may involve complex projects with multiple distribution systems, large services, panelboards, switchboards, transformers, motor loads, emergency systems, fire alarm systems, special occupancies, hazardous locations, lighting systems, and extensive plans and specifications. Successful preparation requires more than memorizing isolated Code rules. Candidates should understand how multiple NEC articles work together and how to apply those requirements to realistic project conditions. The North Carolina Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination is open book for the approved technical references. Candidates may use the 2020 NEC and NFPA 72, 2019 Edition, during the examination. The licensing law, Board rules, and NASCLA business reference are included for closed-book preparation and cannot be consulted during the test. This book package provides the technical, legal, and business references needed to build a structured preparation plan for the North Carolina Unlimited Electrical Contractor examination.
