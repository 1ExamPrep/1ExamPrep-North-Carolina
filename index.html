Which North Carolina board licenses you?

North Carolina splits contractor licensing between three independent boards. The Licensing Board for General Contractors (NCLBGC) covers building, residential, highway, utility and specialty contractors on projects of $40,000 or more. The State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors covers electrical contracting. The State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors covers plumbing, HVAC, fuel piping and fire sprinklers.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions North Carolina candidates ask us most, checked against the state boards and statutes on November 24, 2026.

Do I need a contractor license in North Carolina?
Yes, for general contracting on any project that costs $40,000 or more under G.S. 87-1. Electrical, plumbing, heating and fire sprinkler contractors need a license from their own board.
What score passes the NCLBGC exams?
70 percent on the trade exam and on the Business and Law exam. Both are open book with the approved references.
What are the NCLBGC license limitations?
Limited covers single projects up to $750,000, Intermediate up to $1,500,000, and Unlimited has no project limit.
Does North Carolina accept the NASCLA exam?
Yes. The NCLBGC accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building exam for the Building classification, and the electrical board accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam with a North Carolina rules test.
What is the difference between Heating Group 2 and Group 3?
Heating Group 2 covers air conditioning systems greater than 15 tons. Heating Group 3 covers forced-air heating and cooling of 15 tons or less.
What does a Class II plumbing license cover?
Class II covers plumbing and heating systems in single-family detached homes. Class I covers all structures.
