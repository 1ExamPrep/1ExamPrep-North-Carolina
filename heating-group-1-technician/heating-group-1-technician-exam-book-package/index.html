How does it fit the North Carolina Heating Group 1 Technician exam?

The Heating Group 1 Technician license is for hydronic heating technicians working under a contractor or for a government or institution. Technician Class I needs 18 months (3,000 hours) of experience and Class II needs 15 months (2,500 hours).

Candidates for this license prepare for 1 exam step:

  • Board Heating Group 1 Technician exam: Trade knowledge, North Carolina codes, business and law (Allowed references in the room, 7 NC testing centers)

What will you study with it?

A Class II Heating Group 1 Technician is limited to the applicable work in single-family detached dwellings and must also serve as a sublicensee of a Heating Group 1 contractor. A technician credential is not a contracting license. The package includes the North Carolina Administration Code and Policies, Fuel Gas Code, Mechanical Code, Energy Conservation Code, Residential Code, and the Board’s Laws & Rules. These publications address enforceable code provisions, permitting, inspection procedures, fuel-gas systems, mechanical equipment, energy performance, residential installations, technician qualifications, supervision, and license responsibilities. The technical references expand preparation beyond code lookup. Manual J and Manual N support residential and small-commercial load calculations. Manual D and Manual Q address residential and commercial duct-system design. Manual CS provides guidance on HVAC applications, systems, and equipment.
