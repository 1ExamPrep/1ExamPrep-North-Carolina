What is in the NASCLA Residential Electrician - Highlighted and Tabbed Book Package?

The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. North Carolina accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination in lieu of the North Carolina SP-SFD Single-Family Detached Residential Dwelling examination. Applicants using this pathway must have their NASCLA examination transcript sent to the North Carolina Board as part of the licensing application process. The corresponding North Carolina electrical contractor classification is the Special Restricted Single-Family Residential Dwelling, or SP-SFD, classification. This license is designed for electrical contracting work in or on single-family residential dwellings and qualifying structures associated with those dwellings. Passing the NASCLA examination can satisfy the accepted trade-examination component, but candidates must still meet North Carolina's experience, application, character, and licensing requirements. How does it fit the North Carolina Single Family Detached Dwelling (SP-SFD) exam?

The SP-SFD special restricted license covers electrical work on single-family detached homes. The NC electrical board accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor exam in place of the SP-SFD exam, so both study paths are listed here.

Candidates for this license prepare for 2 exam steps:

  • Board SP-SFD exam: NEC rules for single-family detached homes (Given by the electrical board)
  • NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor exam: National residential electrical exam (Accepted in place of the SP-SFD exam)

What will you study with it?

Exam Details The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program provides standardized electrical trade examinations for participating licensing agencies. The program currently includes examinations for Electrical Contractors or Master Electricians, Journeyman Electricians, and Residential Electrical Contractors. North Carolina currently accepts the NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination specifically for applicants seeking eligibility toward the SP-SFD Special Restricted Single-Family Residential Dwelling classification. The North Carolina Board also accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination for several broader electrical contractor classifications. The NASCLA Residential Electrical Contractor examination is designed around electrical work typically encountered in permanent dwelling units. Preparation should include a combination of technical knowledge, code navigation, safety, calculations, electrical theory, equipment, residential construction, and project-related topics. Candidates using the NASCLA pathway should therefore understand that passing the accepted trade examination is one part of a broader licensing process.
