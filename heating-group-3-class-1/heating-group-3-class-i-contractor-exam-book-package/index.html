How does it fit the North Carolina Heating Group 3 Class I exam?

Heating Group 3 covers forced-air heating and cooling systems of 15 tons or less, and Class I allows that work in any building. Group 3 is the most common HVAC license in North Carolina. The exam covers duct design, heat pumps, fuel gas, venting and the mechanical code.

Candidates for this license prepare for 1 exam step:

  • Board Heating Group 3 Class I exam: Trade knowledge, North Carolina codes, business and law (Allowed references in the room, 7 NC testing centers)

What will you study with it?

The North Carolina Fuel Gas Code covers fuel piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, regulators, shutoff valves, and system testing. The North Carolina Mechanical Code addresses heating, cooling, ventilation, refrigeration, duct systems, equipment locations, installation requirements, and mechanical safety. The North Carolina Energy Conservation Code supports study of equipment efficiency, HVAC controls, duct sealing, insulation, piping requirements, system performance, and other energy-related provisions. The North Carolina Residential Code provides requirements affecting heating, cooling, mechanical, and fuel-gas installations in one- and two-family dwellings and townhouses within its scope. The NC Board’s Laws & Rules addresses contractor classifications, applications, examinations, supervision, professional conduct, and licensing responsibilities. The HVAC design references add practical calculation and system-design material. Manual J supports residential load calculations, while Manual N addresses load calculations for small commercial buildings.
