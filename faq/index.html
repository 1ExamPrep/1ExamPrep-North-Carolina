North Carolina Contractor Exam FAQ

North Carolina contractor exams are reference-book tests run by three boards. NCLBGC applicants need 70 percent on a trade exam and the Business and Law exam, while electrical and plumbing candidates sit their own board's exam. The answers below cover exams, licenses and ordering from us.

Last updated November 24, 2026

What should you know about the exams?

  • PSI gives the NCLBGC trade and Business and Law exams.
  • The electrical board gives its own written exams, and accepts NASCLA with a rules test.
  • The Plumbing Board tests daily at 7 centers across North Carolina.
  • Approved references are allowed in the room, so book editions matter.

Which license do you need?

The trade and the value of the work decide it. General contracting on a project of $40,000 or more needs an NCLBGC license in the right classification, such as Building, Residential, Highway or Roofing. Electrical contracting needs a Limited, Intermediate, Unlimited or special restricted license from the electrical board. Plumbing needs Class I for all buildings or Class II for single-family detached homes, and HVAC needs Heating Group 1, 2 or 3 from the Plumbing Board. Fuel gas piping and fire sprinkler contractors also license through the Plumbing Board. The license list groups every license we cover by board.

How long should you study?

Most candidates need 4 to 8 weeks. Plan for more if you work full time or take a trade exam and Business and Law in the same month. A good split is 2 weeks learning the layout of each reference book, 2 to 4 weeks of practice questions, and a final 1 to 2 weeks of timed full-length exams. Because these exams allow reference books, the goal is fast lookups rather than memorizing. Read the licensing guide for the full process.

What should you bring on exam day?

Bring a government photo ID and the approval or admission notice from your board. For the NCLBGC exams at PSI and for the Plumbing Board exams, bring every allowed reference book in the edition on the reference list. Tabs must be permanently attached, and loose notes or papers tucked inside the books are not allowed. Arrive early so the books can be checked before you start. Check your board's candidate information for the exact list, because rules on calculators and highlighting can differ by exam.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions North Carolina candidates ask us most, checked against the state boards and statutes on November 24, 2026.

When do I need a general contractor license?
When a project costs $40,000 or more, under G.S. 87-1.
What score passes the NCLBGC exams?
70 percent on both the trade exam and the Business and Law exam.
What are the NCLBGC limitations?
Limited up to $750,000 per project, Intermediate up to $1,500,000 and Unlimited with no limit.
What working capital does each limitation need?
$17,000 for Limited, $75,000 for Intermediate and $150,000 for Unlimited, or a $175,000, $500,000 or $1,000,000 bond.
What are the electrical license limits?
The statute caps Limited at $100,000 and 1,000 volts and Intermediate at $200,000. Unlimited has no cap.
Where does the Plumbing Board test?
Asheville, Charlotte, Winston-Salem, Raleigh, Fayetteville, Greenville and Wilmington, daily.
