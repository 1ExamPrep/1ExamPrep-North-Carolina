How to Get a North Carolina Contractor License

To get a North Carolina contractor license, pick the board that covers your trade, apply with proof of experience or finances, then pass that board's exams. General contractors need an NCLBGC license for projects of $40,000 or more and pass a trade exam and Business and Law at 70 percent.

Last updated November 24, 2026

Key takeaways

  • General contractor license required for projects of $40,000 or more.
  • NCLBGC limits: $750,000, $1,500,000 or unlimited.
  • NCLBGC exams: 70% to pass, open book.
  • Electrical caps: $100,000 Limited, $200,000 Intermediate by statute.
  • Plumbing Board: 7 testing centers; 90 days to test after notice.

What are the steps to a North Carolina contractor license?

  1. Pick the board. General contracting goes to the NCLBGC, electrical work to the electrical board, and plumbing, HVAC, fuel piping and fire sprinkler work to the Plumbing Board.
  2. Choose the classification. Match your work to a classification such as Building, Residential, Unlimited Electrical or Heating Group 3.
  3. Apply to the board. Send the application with proof of experience, and for the NCLBGC, working capital or a bond for your limitation.
  4. Pass the exams. Take the trade exam and, for the NCLBGC, the Business and Law exam. Bring the allowed reference books.
  5. Get licensed and renew. Receive the license, set up the business and keep up with yearly renewal and continuing education.

Most candidates finish the exams within 4 to 8 weeks of starting to study. Board review time depends on how complete the application is.

Which board covers your work?

North Carolina uses three independent boards, each set up by a different article of Chapter 87 of the General Statutes. Article 1 covers general contractors, Article 2 covers plumbing, heating and fire sprinkler contractors, and Article 4 covers electrical contractors.

BoardWho needs itExams

Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler boardPlumbing, HVAC, fuel piping and fire sprinkler contractors and techniciansBoard exam at 7 testing centers

A general contractor license does not let you do electrical, plumbing or HVAC work. Those trades need their own board's license even on a small job.

How do NCLBGC limitations work?

Every NCLBGC license carries a limitation that caps the value of a single project, excluding land. You qualify with working capital, or with a surety bond as the alternative, and the application fee rises with the tier.

LimitationSingle project limitWorking capitalBond alternative
Limited$750,000$17,000$175,000
Intermediate$1,500,000$75,000$500,000
UnlimitedNo limit$150,000$1,000,000

Application fees are $75 for Limited, $100 for Intermediate and $125 for Unlimited under G.S. 87-10.

How does the electrical license work?

The State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors issues Limited, Intermediate and Unlimited licenses plus special restricted classes such as SP-SFD, SP-FA/LV and SP-PH. G.S. 87-43.3 caps the Limited project value at $100,000 on installations of 1,000 volts or less and the Intermediate value at $200,000, with the Board setting the working limits within those caps. Limited and SP applicants need up to 3,000 hours of experience, and Intermediate applicants up to 5,750 hours. The Board accepts the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam as an alternative, as long as you also get 18 of 25 right on the North Carolina Laws, Rules and Business Practices test.

How does the Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler license work?

G.S. 87-21 sets two classes: Class I covers all structures, and Class II covers single-family detached homes. Heating is split into groups. Group 1 is hydronic heating, Group 2 is air conditioning greater than 15 tons, and Group 3 is forced-air heating and cooling. Contractor applicants need 2 years (4,000 hours) of experience. The Board tests daily at 7 centers: Asheville, Charlotte, Winston-Salem, Raleigh, Fayetteville, Greenville and Wilmington. You take the exam within 90 days of notice and wait 90 days to retest after a failed attempt.

How should you study for a reference-book exam?

  • Get the exact edition of every book on your exam's reference list.
  • Tab and highlight the sections that answer most questions, or buy them pre-tabbed.
  • Practice timed questions so lookups become automatic.
  • Take full practice exams in the last 2 weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions North Carolina candidates ask us most, checked against the state boards and statutes on November 24, 2026.

How long does it take to get a North Carolina contractor license?
Most candidates study 4 to 8 weeks. Board review adds time before and after the exam, depending on the board and the application.
How much does an NCLBGC license cost?
The application fee is $75 for Limited, $100 for Intermediate and $125 for Unlimited, plus PSI exam fees and the cost of showing working capital or a bond.
Do I need a license for work under $40,000?
Not a general contractor license under G.S. 87-1. Electrical, plumbing, heating and fire sprinkler work still needs a license from those boards.
Do I need experience?
The electrical and plumbing boards set experience hours by license, such as 4,000 hours for a plumbing or heating contractor. The NCLBGC checks your qualifications and finances.
Can a company hold the license?
Yes. Licenses are issued to the business, with a qualifier who passed the exams.
What is the NASCLA exam?
A national exam that North Carolina accepts for the NCLBGC Building classification and, with a rules test, for electrical contractors.
Which board licenses HVAC contractors?
The Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler board, through Heating Groups 1, 2 and 3.
